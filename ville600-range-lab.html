How to use this before the Ville 600

You can't memorise hundreds of charts as pictures. You can memorise a few dozen edges and how they move with stack depth. Everything in this tool is built around that.

  1. Learn the shape, not 169 cells

    Every range is a handful of ladders: pairs, Ax suited, Kx offsuit and so on. In each ladder you only need the edge, the weakest hand still in. The Edges panel on Chip EV Charts gives you those edges for every spot.

  2. Learn in steps, not whole charts

    Pick a stack you know well, say 25bb, and step the stack slider down one notch at a time, watching the thumbnails. Only a few hands change at each step, and in a predictable order: small pairs and weak suited aces turn into shoves first, offsuit broadways last.

  3. File it under the hand

    At the table you look at K9o before you think about charts. Hand lens shows one hand in every seat at every stack, and the Numbers drill turns it into rules like “CO: ≤13 Jam · 14–40 Raise”. Learn those rules for the 25–30 hands that keep changing; the rest are always played or always folded.

  4. Recall it, don't re-read it

    Re-reading charts mostly trains recognition. Painting a range from memory and deciding fast on edge hands trains recall, which is what you use at the table. Drill picks edge hands and brings your misses back on a spaced schedule.

  5. Add ICM last

    Lock in the Chip EV ranges first, then learn ICM as the hands that drop out. The bubble and final table mostly cut calls and marginal opens; shoving ranges change less than calling ranges.

Two-week plan

Day 1–2First in, 25–40bb. Chip EV Charts, stepping the stack slider down from 40bb, then Paint three seats a day and Quick-fire “First in”, 21–50bb.
Deep stacksFor the opening levels: the “Deep stacks · 50–100” preset in Quick-fire, then Paint CO and BTN opens and BB vs BTN at 60 and 100bb.
Day 3–4First in, 10–20bb, where raising and shoving split. Hand lens on the hands that switch, then the Numbers drill.
Day 5Under 10bb: shove or fold. Quick-fire 5–9bb plus Numbers.
Day 6–7Big blind: defending against opens and calling shoves. Paint BB vs BTN, CO and SB at 12, 18 and 25bb.
Day 8–9Facing opens: reshoves and 3-bets, then calling off when you open and get shoved on. Edges drill.
Day 10–11ICM Stage Charts, then Quick-fire with the ICM solutions.
Day 12–13Only the reddest squares in your “Where you leak” map.
Day 14Light review. Hand lens on the hands you miss most. No new material.

Every day: 10 minutes of Paint or Edges, then 15 minutes of Quick-fire. Reviews that are due come first automatically.

At the table

Run the same order every hand: stack in bb → seat → hand → that hand's number. Drill in that order too: read the stack before the cards.

After a session

Rebuild the hands you weren't sure about in Spot builder: tap the seats in the order they acted, then slide the stack. Do it on a break or after play; live events don't allow checking charts during a hand.

About the numbers

Chip EV Charts, Hand lens and the default drills use 9-handed MTT solutions with a 12.5% big-blind ante, Chip EV, and equal stacks. ICM Stage Charts use 8-handed ICM solutions for a 1,000-player field. Real stacks are rarely equal, so treat these as anchors and adjust when the stacks behind you are much shorter or deeper.
