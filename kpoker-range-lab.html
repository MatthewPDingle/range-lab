How to study K Poker ranges

K Poker plays 7-handed with an ante from every seat and a small blind as big as the big blind, so the pot starts at 9bb before anyone acts. Limping is common and opens are big, and the open size changes everything after it.

  1. Start from the hand

    At the table you see your cards first. Hand lens shows one hand in every common spot, at every open size: first in from each seat, against limpers, against an open, after you limped and someone raised, and in 3-bet and 4-bet pots.

  2. Learn the open size as a slider

    Pick the open size your game uses, then step it up and down one notch on Charts, watching the thumbnails. Only a few hands change at each step, so you learn the other sizes as changes rather than new charts.

  3. Each line is its own spot

    Many lines lead to the same seat matchup (who limped before the raise, who called after). Charts lists every line for your seat with how often it comes up; Hand lens shows the most common one.

  4. Multiway pots are estimates

    When two or more players are already in, the solver values the flop with a learned estimate rather than solving it out. Those spots carry a corner mark: learn the shape, but trust them less than heads-up spots.

  5. Frequencies only

    The solver gives how often each hand takes each action, not what each choice is worth. A hand split between two actions is close to indifferent between them.

Suggested order

Step 1First in at your game's open size: limp, raise or fold from each seat. Charts, then Hand lens on the hands that mix.
